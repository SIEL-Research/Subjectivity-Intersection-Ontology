2. Subjectivity Intersection Ontology

## 2.1 Status of This Chapter

This chapter defines the basic structure of Subjectivity Intersection Ontology (SIO) in its own terms. Its purpose is neither to translate SIO into concepts drawn from existing philosophical traditions nor to discuss its similarities to other ontologies. Historical comparison and philosophical interpretation are reserved for subsequent chapters.

SIO does not reduce subjectivity to individual psychology, cognitive function, or observational error. It treats subjectivity itself as a constituent of ontology. The account presented in this chapter is not an empirically established fact, but an ontological proposition open to comparison, formalization, and future examination.

The definition of SIO proceeds in the following order:

1. triadic self-closure within primordial unity;
2. Absolute Subjectivity, Relative Subjectivity, and Intersection Subjectivity;
3. projection from Absolute Subjectivity to Relative Subjectivity;
4. the emergence of Intersection Subjectivity in the interval between Absolute Subjectivity and Relative Subjectivity;
5. hierarchical repetition among multiple Relative Subjectivities; and
6. the distinction among ontological propositions, formal representations, and empirical consequences.

These components are related, but they do not belong to the same level. In particular, the ontological prototype consisting of Absolute Subjectivity, Relative Subjectivity, and Intersection Subjectivity must be distinguished from the operational form `AB → C → AB`, which is used in relation to multiple Relative Subjectivities.

## 2.2 Ontology

In SIO, subjectivity is defined as perspective. Hereafter, the terms “subjectivity” and “perspective” are used synonymously. Accordingly, A, B, and C in this section are all subjectivities—that is, perspectives.

SIO posits one primordial existence. This unity, however, is not a static identity devoid of difference or relation. A single existence may internally contain a perspective A that gives itself to itself, a perspective B that receives itself from itself, and a perspective C that arises as the intersection of A and B.

```text
One existence S
├─ A: the perspective that gives itself to itself
├─ B: the perspective that receives itself from itself
└─ C: the third perspective that closes the intersection of A and B
```

A, B, and C are not three independently pre-existing entities. They are three mutually irreducible perspectives internal to one existence. SIO therefore does not posit three independent primordial existences; in this limited sense, it adopts monism.

C is not a result externally added after A and B have already been completed. The constitution of A as the giving perspective and B as the receiving perspective already includes the constitution of C, which completes their correspondence as a single relation.

C is therefore both the result of the intersection of A and B and the condition under which that intersection can exist as a unified relation.

```text
A and B constitute C
C constitutes the correspondence between A and B as a single closure
```

This interdependence is not a temporal cycle consisting of an antecedent cause and a subsequent effect. It is an ontological closure in which A, B, and C exist simultaneously as a single self-consistent relation.

Through this triadic self-closure, further differences, correspondences, relations, and interactions can arise from a single existence. This dynamic extension does not occur through the external addition of a second entity to an undifferentiated one. It becomes possible through giving, receiving, and their intersection, all of which are internal to one existence.

## 2.3 Absolute Subjectivity

Absolute Subjectivity is not an observable object. If a third perspective were positioned outside Absolute Subjectivity in order to observe it, Absolute Subjectivity would already have become an object of observation, contrary to its definition.

Absolute Subjectivity is neither an observational point located within the universe nor a hypothetical perspective surveying everything from outside. It is the primordial subjective pole through which phenomena and perspectives can exist.

In this chapter, Absolute Subjectivity is represented by the symbol \(S\). This symbol does not transform Absolute Subjectivity itself into an observable object. \(S\) is a notation used to describe the ontological structure of SIO.

## 2.4 Relative Subjectivity

Relative Subjectivity is a subjectivity that receives the projection of Absolute Subjectivity and becomes differentiated as a perspective. A single Relative Subjectivity is represented by \(R\); multiple Relative Subjectivities are distinguished as \(R_1, R_2, \ldots, R_n\).

Coordinates such as body, location, time, utterance, and action may be assigned to a Relative Subjectivity. These coordinates, however, are not Relative Subjectivity itself. What can be observed are its expressions, traces, position, actions, and records. The subjectivity experiencing the world cannot itself be observed externally as an object.

The following distinctions must therefore be maintained:

```text
Relative Subjectivity ≠ the coordinates of Relative Subjectivity
Relative Subjectivity ≠ the observable expressions of Relative Subjectivity
```

Relative Subjectivity is not identical to Absolute Subjectivity. Each Relative Subjectivity receives a projection of Absolute Subjectivity, but lives that projection partially and relatively from a particular perspective.

## 2.5 Intersection Subjectivity

Intersection Subjectivity is a subjectivity that emerges in the interval between Absolute Subjectivity and a Relative Subjectivity differentiated through its projection. In the ontological prototype, Intersection Subjectivity is represented by \(X\).

Intersection Subjectivity cannot be reduced to either Absolute Subjectivity or Relative Subjectivity. Nor is it their average, agreement, fusion, or shared part. It is an irreducible third subjective pole that arises when the correspondence between two subjective poles is completed as a single relation.

The constitution of Intersection Subjectivity does not imply a temporal sequence in which two perspectives first intersect and a third subjectivity is subsequently produced in a separate process. The intersection of the perspectives and the constitution of Intersection Subjectivity are simultaneous. Through their intersection, Absolute Subjectivity, Relative Subjectivity, and Intersection Subjectivity exist as a single integrated structure.

Intersection Subjectivity itself is also unobservable. What may be observed are the consequences accompanying its constitution, including understanding, synchronization, action, meaning, relational change, and co-generation.

## 2.6 Projection

Let \(S\) denote Absolute Subjectivity, \(R_i\) a Relative Subjectivity, and \(\pi_i\) a projection. Their relation may be represented as follows:

\[
S \xrightarrow{\pi_i} R_i
\]

Projection does not mean a mapping between two already observed objects. It represents the structure through which unobservable Absolute Subjectivity appears as a Relative Subjectivity possessing a particular perspective.

Different Relative Subjectivities receive different projections:

\[
S \xrightarrow{\pi_1} R_1
\]

\[
S \xrightarrow{\pi_2} R_2
\]

Each Relative Subjectivity receives a projection of Absolute Subjectivity, but lives that projection from a different perspective. This difference does not arise through the introduction of a second primordial entity external to Absolute Subjectivity. It arises through the projection by which differentiated Relative Subjectivities and perspectives are constituted.

Projection indicates the relation from Absolute Subjectivity to Relative Subjectivity and is one component of the ontological prototype consisting of Absolute Subjectivity, Relative Subjectivity, and Intersection Subjectivity.

## 2.7 The Ontological Prototype

Let \(S\) denote Absolute Subjectivity and \(R\) a Relative Subjectivity differentiated through its projection. The ontological prototype of SIO is defined as follows:

> When Relative Subjectivity \(R\) is differentiated through the projection of Absolute Subjectivity \(S\), an Intersection Subjectivity \(X\), reducible to neither, emerges in the interval between Absolute Subjectivity \(S\) and the projected Relative Subjectivity \(R\).

This structure may be represented schematically as follows:

```text
Absolute Subjectivity S ──π──▶ Relative Subjectivity R
             Intersection Subjectivity X emerges
                    in the interval between S and R
```

This projection is not an event in which a second entity inexplicably proceeds from an undifferentiated one. It expresses the triadic self-closure internal to primordial unity as the relation among Absolute Subjectivity, Relative Subjectivity, and Intersection Subjectivity.

Absolute Subjectivity as the perspective that gives itself, Relative Subjectivity as the perspective that receives its projection and lives a particular perspective, and Intersection Subjectivity as the subjectivity that completes their correspondence together form a single ontological structure.

This is not an operational definition in which a third variable is calculated from two already observed objects. It is an ontological definition specifying the relation through which Absolute Subjectivity, Relative Subjectivity, and Intersection Subjectivity exist.

## 2.8 Extension to Multiple Subjectivities

Relative Subjectivities \(R_1\) and \(R_2\), receiving multiple projections of Absolute Subjectivity, possess different perspectives and histories. The constitution of Intersection Subjectivity in the interval between these Relative Subjectivities repeats, at the level of Relative Subjectivity, the ontological prototype in which Intersection Subjectivity emerges between Absolute Subjectivity and Relative Subjectivity.

In the multiple-subjectivity extension, perspective intersection does not occur merely when one Relative Subjectivity apprehends another as an object within its own perspective. SIO calls the change in the standpoint from which the other is apprehended a perspective transformation. Perspective intersection is defined through a transformation in which one Relative Subjectivity views the other from the other’s perspective.

```text
V₁(R₂) ──perspective transformation──▶ V₂(R₂)
```

Let \(A\) and \(B\) denote two Relative Subjectivities and \(C\) the Intersection Subjectivity constituted in their interval. The structure may be abbreviated as follows:

```text
AB → C → AB
```

This notation does not represent a simple temporal sequence. The constitution of \(C\) completes the relation between \(A\) and \(B\) as a whole. Within this relational unity, \(A\), \(B\), and \(C\) exist simultaneously.

Intersection Subjectivity \(C\) is not a third party added externally to \(A\) and \(B\). It is a subjectivity reducible to neither, constituted when the intersection of perspectives completes the relation as a whole.

The constituted intersection acts upon the perspectives, histories, actions, and interactions of \(A\) and \(B\). The initial difference can thereby give rise to new differences, correspondences, relations, and interactions.

`AB → C → AB` is not a definition that replaces the ontological prototype. It is the canonical operational form through which that prototype is represented in relation to multiple Relative Subjectivities.

## 2.9 Boundary Conditions

To clarify the scope of SIO, the boundary between its ontological claims and observable consequences must be specified.

First, Absolute Subjectivity, Relative Subjectivity, and Intersection Subjectivity are not themselves objects of observation. What may be observed in relation to Relative Subjectivity includes coordinates, body, utterance, action, records, and expressions. What may be observed in relation to Intersection Subjectivity includes traces of understanding, synchronization, mutual change, co-generation, and relational completion accompanying its constitution.

| Subjectivity | Observability | What May Be Observed |
|---|---|---|
| Absolute Subjectivity | Unobservable | Structural consequences if projection is assumed |
| Relative Subjectivity | Subjectivity itself is unobservable | Coordinates, body, utterances, actions, records, expressions |
| Intersection Subjectivity | Subjectivity itself is unobservable | New understanding, synchronization, mutual change, co-generation, traces of relational completion |

Unobservability does not entail non-existence. Nevertheless, unobservable subjectivity itself must be distinguished from the observable consequences predicted to accompany its constitution.

Second, Intersection Subjectivity is not defined as an average, consensus, or fusion of Relative Subjectivities. Nor can it be explained solely as a common part upon a shared basis or as one subjectivity’s imitation of another. Such phenomena may accompany the constitution of Intersection Subjectivity, but they cannot be identified with Intersection Subjectivity itself.

Third, an observable proxy variable, a mathematical intermediary, or a third node in a computational model is not Intersection Subjectivity itself. These are means of representing the consequences or relational structures predicted if Intersection Subjectivity is constituted.

Fourth, `AB → C → AB` among multiple Relative Subjectivities does not replace the ontological prototype as a whole. The prototype of SIO consists in the projection from Absolute Subjectivity to Relative Subjectivity and the emergence of Intersection Subjectivity in their interval. The multiple-subjectivity form extends this prototype to the level of Relative Subjectivities.

## 2.10 Distinguishing the Ontological, Formal, and Empirical Levels

At least three levels must be distinguished when discussing SIO.

### 2.10.1 Ontological Level

The ontological level concerns propositions about the constitution of Absolute Subjectivity, Relative Subjectivity, and Intersection Subjectivity.

Its central proposition is as follows:

> The ontological prototype of subjectivity intersection is that, when Relative Subjectivity is differentiated through the projection of Absolute Subjectivity, an Intersection Subjectivity reducible to neither emerges in the interval between Absolute Subjectivity and the projected Relative Subjectivity.

This is the definition of SIO. It is not a conclusion derived from a mathematical model or empirical result.

### 2.10.2 Formal Level

The formal level represents projection, perspective transformation, relational completion, and extension to multiple subjectivities through symbols or mathematical structures.

Possible candidates for formalization include projections, perspective transformations, commutative diagrams, operators, simultaneous fixed points, closure, repetition, and holonomy. These mathematical objects, however, are not subjectivities themselves. The role of formalization is to distinguish the structures hypothesized to hold among unobservable subjective poles from their observable consequences and to specify the correspondence between the two.

### 2.10.3 Empirical Level

The empirical level concerns the observable consequences that would be expected if Intersection Subjectivity were constituted. At least the following elements must be specified separately:

1. **Ontological proposition**
   What is assumed concerning Absolute Subjectivity, Relative Subjectivity, and Intersection Subjectivity.

2. **Operator hypothesis**
   How projection, perspective transformation, intersection, and action upon the relation are formally represented.

3. **Observable consequences**
   What is measured, including synchronization, mutual prediction, new meaning, co-generation, or simultaneous changes in both participants.

4. **Decision rule**
   What results would count as support, non-support, or indeterminacy.

If an appropriate proxy cannot be constructed, the relevant study cannot be regarded as having refuted SIO. The boundary must instead be preserved by classifying the proposition as not presently testable.

These distinctions prevent the success of a mathematical model from being treated as proof of the ontology, an observable third variable from being identified with Intersection Subjectivity itself, or an empirical correlation from being regarded as direct evidence for the existence of subjectivity.

## 2.11 Summary and Ontological Motivation

The primordial existence posited by SIO is one, but it is not without difference. A single existence internally contains, as a self-consistent structure, a perspective that gives itself to itself, a perspective that receives itself from itself, and a third perspective that is both the result of their intersection and the condition under which that intersection can exist.

This triadic self-closure is expressed as the ontological prototype consisting of Absolute Subjectivity, Relative Subjectivity, and Intersection Subjectivity:

```text
Absolute Subjectivity S ──projection──▶ Relative Subjectivity R
                  Intersection Subjectivity X emerges
                         in the interval between S and R
```

Subjectivity intersection among multiple Relative Subjectivities repeats this prototype at the level of Relative Subjectivity:

```text
AB → C → AB
```

Here, \(A\) and \(B\) are differentiated Relative Subjectivities, and \(C\) is the Intersection Subjectivity constituted in their interval. This form does not replace the ontological prototype. It is the canonical operational form for addressing perspectives, histories, interactions, and observable consequences.

SIO defines the intersection of perspectives as constituting a third subjectivity irreducible to the subjectivities from which it emerges. The intersection completes the relation as a whole, from which further differences, correspondences, relations, and interactions can arise.

SIO addresses the question of how unity, difference, and relation can be accounted for within the same ontological structure.

If the unity of primordial existence alone is taken as the starting proposition, a further account is required of how difference arises and why correspondence or relation can exist between differences. Conversely, if multiple independent entities are posited from the outset, the question remains as to why they can enter into relation with one another.

SIO takes neither a temporal transition from one to two nor the external conjunction of two independent entities as its starting point. It proposes as its prototype a triadic self-closure in which one existence simultaneously contains perspective A, which gives itself; perspective B, which receives itself; and perspective C, which completes their intersection. Difference and relation are not added to this structure from outside but are internal to its constitution.

The ontological motivation for introducing SIO is therefore not to reject monism or dualism in general. It is to present an explicit structure capable of describing the constitution of difference while preserving unity and the constitution of relation while preserving difference.

Whether this structure is more adequate than other ontologies, and whether it is historically distinct, cannot be concluded from the definitions presented in this chapter alone. The following chapters therefore evaluate SIO not by redefining its ontology, but by comparing the ontology defined here with existing ontological traditions and by clarifying its relation to possible formal and empirical investigation.
