# Participatory Sense-Making and Subjectivity Intersection Ontology

## Status

**Provisional verdict:**

> **VERY STRONG INTERACTIONAL PRECEDENT, NOT AN EXACT MATCH ON CURRENT EVIDENCE**

Participatory sense-making is among the closest contemporary precedents to Subjectivity Intersection Ontology (SIO). It rejects the idea that social understanding is merely the exchange of internally completed representations and instead treats interaction as a partially autonomous, dynamically organized process that can shape the participants themselves.

Its strongest overlap with SIO is therefore not simply “intersubjectivity.” It is the stronger claim that the relation can acquire organization, history, constraints, and causal efficacy that are not reducible to either participant considered alone.

The decisive unresolved question is whether this interaction process is ever granted irreducible **subjective status**, rather than autonomy, normativity, meaning-generating capacity, or dynamical organization.

---

## 1. Review target

This review audits the enactive and interactional tradition associated with participatory sense-making, especially the framework developed by Hanne De Jaegher and Ezequiel Di Paolo, together with later work on interactive autonomy, social agency, coordination, affective interaction, and collective sense-making.

The comparison is made against the six novelty criteria defined in this repository:

- **N1 — Joint generation**
- **N2 — History irreducibility**
- **N3 — Irreducible third subjectivity**
- **N4 — Constitutive mediation**
- **N5 — Return action**
- **N6 — Cross-scale structural recurrence**

The review must distinguish carefully among:

1. jointly generated meaning,
2. an autonomous interaction process,
3. a relational system with causal efficacy,
4. a collective or dyadic agent,
5. an irreducible third subjectivity.

These are not equivalent claims.

---

## 2. Core structure of participatory sense-making

Participatory sense-making begins from the enactive view that cognition is not passive information processing but embodied sense-making: an autonomous organism enacts a meaningful world through its precarious self-maintaining activity.

The social extension adds a second level. When two autonomous agents interact, their coordination may develop a dynamic organization that is not under the unilateral control of either participant. The interaction can acquire its own tendencies, rhythms, breakdowns, recoveries, and constraints.

A minimal schematic representation is:

```text
A: autonomous sense-maker
B: autonomous sense-maker

A ↔ B
  ↓
interaction process I
  ↓
constraints and affordances upon A and B
  ↓
modified individual and joint sense-making
```

This is already stronger than a model in which A and B merely transmit pre-existing meanings. Meaning can be generated through participation in the interaction itself.

---

## 3. Why this is a strongest-counterexample candidate

Participatory sense-making is a serious counterexample candidate because it supports several claims that weaker theories of intersubjectivity do not.

### 3.1 The interaction is not reducible to two independent monologues

The interaction can develop patterns that neither participant independently designed or controls. This gives the relation a degree of operational autonomy.

### 3.2 Meaning can be generated in the interaction

The participants do not merely reveal private meanings. New significance can emerge through timing, coordination, interruption, repair, resistance, affective attunement, and the unfolding history of the encounter.

### 3.3 The interaction can constrain its participants

Once established, an interaction pattern may enable, solicit, inhibit, or redirect the actions of both participants. This is a strong analogue of return action.

### 3.4 The history of the encounter matters

The present organization of the interaction may depend on the path through which it developed. The same individuals placed into a different interaction history need not generate the same relational process.

### 3.5 The framework crosses levels

Related interactional structures are discussed at bodily, affective, conversational, institutional, and collective levels. This creates a plausible route toward N6.

These features make participatory sense-making substantially closer to SIO than theories that treat the “between” as merely metaphorical.

## 4. Criterion-by-criterion audit

## N1 — Joint generation

**Assessment: strongly supported.**

Participatory sense-making explicitly concerns processes that are generated through the coordination of multiple autonomous agents. The interaction is not attributable to A alone or B alone.

A strong form of N1 is present when:

- both agents contribute to the interaction,
- the resulting pattern depends on their reciprocal coupling,
- neither agent can produce the same process in isolation,
- the interaction develops organization not reducible to either individual trajectory.

This is one of the clearest areas of overlap with SIO.

### Limitation

Joint generation of a process does not by itself establish joint generation of a subject. N1 can be satisfied while N3 fails.

---

## N2 — History irreducibility

**Assessment: strongly supported in many interactional cases.**

Interactive coordination is frequently path-dependent. The meaning and organization of a present exchange may depend on prior turns, mutual adjustments, failed coordinations, repairs, habits, and sedimented expectations.

The interaction history can therefore be constitutive rather than merely descriptive.

This supports N2 when the state of the interaction cannot be reconstructed from a static snapshot of the two participants alone.

### Limitation

The literature must still be audited for an explicit claim of irreducibility rather than ordinary causal dependence. Strong N2 requires that the history cannot be eliminated without changing the identity or organization of the generated relational process.

---

## N3 — Irreducible third subjectivity

**Assessment: not established on current evidence.**

This is the decisive gap.

Participatory sense-making grants the interaction process forms of autonomy, normativity, organization, and causal influence. It may also motivate talk of dyadic agency, collective agency, or a “we.” But none of these automatically establishes an irreducible third subjectivity.

The following claims remain distinct:

```text
The interaction has autonomous dynamics.
The interaction generates meaning.
The dyad can act as a unit.
The participants experience a we-relation.
A third subject of experience exists.
```

Only the final claim would directly satisfy N3.

The interaction process may be more than the sum of A and B without being a subject that experiences, apprehends, or possesses a perspective of its own.

### Exact-match threshold

N3 would require evidence that the generated relational unity is not merely:

- a coordination pattern,
- a dynamical system,
- a norm-governed process,
- a shared intentional structure,
- a collective agent,
- a jointly enacted meaning,
- or a phenomenological sense of “we,”

but an irreducible subjectivity constituted through the intersection of A and B.

No such explicit commitment has yet been verified.

---

## N4 — Constitutive mediation

**Assessment: strongly supported structurally.**

In participatory sense-making, the interaction is not merely a product of two already completed agents. Participation in the interaction can partly constitute what each participant perceives, intends, and is able to do within the encounter.

The relational process can therefore mediate the very A–B relation that generates it:

```text
A and B generate I
I structures how A and B can continue to relate
```

This is close to SIO’s closure condition, in which the generated third is not only an output but also a condition for the continued relation of the generating poles.

### Limitation

The constitutive mediator is normally described as an interaction process, coordination dynamics, or participatory domain—not as a third subjectivity.

---

## N5 — Return action

**Assessment: strongly supported at the level of causal constraint.**

The autonomous interaction process can act back upon both participants by:

- stabilizing or destabilizing coordination,
- soliciting responses,
- constraining available actions,
- shaping attention,
- modifying affective orientation,
- reorganizing individual sense-making,
- and sustaining patterns that neither participant can easily terminate alone.

This is one of the strongest contemporary analogues of return action.

### Limitation

SIO requires careful distinction between:

```text
I causally constrains A and B
```

and

```text
C, as an irreducible subjectivity, acts back upon A and B
```

Participatory sense-making strongly supports the first. The second remains unverified.

---

## N6 — Cross-scale structural recurrence

**Assessment: partially supported; exact invariance not established.**

The framework has been extended across multiple levels:

- sensorimotor coordination,
- face-to-face interaction,
- affective engagement,
- conversation,
- joint action,
- social practices,
- institutional settings,
- collective sense-making.

This suggests a recurring relational architecture:

```text
multiple autonomous participants
→ interactional organization
→ emergent constraints and meaning
→ transformed participant activity
```

However, N6 requires more than thematic repetition. It asks whether one sufficiently invariant generative structure recurs across scales.

The literature must therefore be audited for whether the same formal relation is preserved, rather than a family resemblance among different kinds of social organization.

---

## 5. Pair specificity

Participatory sense-making appears strongly compatible with pair specificity.

A concrete interaction process depends on the identities, histories, capacities, timing, and reciprocal adjustments of the particular participants. Replacing either participant may change the resulting coordination and meaning.

This resembles the SIO claim that the intersection subjectivity generated by A and B is specific to that relation and cannot be treated as a generic third term.

Yet pair specificity alone does not establish subjectivity. A pair-specific dynamical pattern can remain non-subjective.

---

## 6. Simultaneity and mutual activity

Participatory sense-making improves significantly upon process models in which predecessors have already become passive data before a new experiential unity forms.

Here A and B remain active autonomous agents during the interaction. Their reciprocal activity jointly sustains the interaction process.

This produces a notably close structural comparison:

```text
Participatory sense-making:
active A ↔ active B
      ↓
autonomous interaction process I
      ↓
I constrains and transforms A and B

SIO:
subjectivity A ↔ subjectivity B
      ↓
irreducible intersection subjectivity C
      ↓
C acts back upon and reconstitutes A and B
```

The main remaining difference is therefore not joint generation, simultaneity, or return influence. It is the ontological status of the generated third.

---

## 7. Interaction autonomy is not yet subjectivity

The most important conceptual safeguard in this review is:

> **Autonomy of an interaction process must not be silently translated into subjectivity of that process.**

In enactive theory, autonomy normally concerns a network of mutually enabling processes that constitutes and maintains an identity under precarious conditions.

An interaction may possess a relative autonomy because its coordinated dynamics become self-sustaining or self-constraining. But a self-maintaining organization is not automatically a subject of experience.

To infer subjectivity, additional criteria would be required, potentially including:

- a first-person or perspectival organization,
- integrated experiential unity,
- self/world differentiation,
- intrinsic normativity attributable to the generated whole,
- temporally continuous self-maintenance,
- or evidence that phenomenology belongs to the relational system itself rather than only to its members.

The current review must not assume these conditions are met.

---

## 8. The “we” problem

Later enactive and phenomenological work sometimes discusses we-experience, shared affect, joint agency, or collective intentionality.

A “we” can mean several different things:

1. each participant represents the pair as “we,”
2. both participants mutually experience belonging together,
3. a joint activity has a unified practical organization,
4. a collective agent exists for explanatory purposes,
5. a numerically distinct subject of experience exists.

Only the fifth would directly support N3.

The review should therefore resist moving from grammatical first-person plural language to an ontological conclusion about a third subject.

---

## 9. Strongest overlap with SIO

The strongest common structure is:

```text
A and B do not merely exchange fixed contents.
Their reciprocal coupling generates a relation-specific process.
That process has a history and partial autonomy.
It shapes what A and B can perceive, mean, and do.
The process therefore returns causal and normative influence to both.
```

This gives participatory sense-making strong provisional support for:

- N1,
- N2,
- N4,
- N5,
- pair specificity,
- simultaneous active participation.

It may provide partial support for N6.

The unresolved criterion is N3.

---

## 10. Decisive difference from SIO

The present boundary can be expressed as follows:

```text
Participatory sense-making:
A + B
→ autonomous interaction process
→ jointly generated meaning
→ transformed A and B

Subjectivity Intersection Ontology:
A + B
→ irreducible subjectivity C
→ C becomes constitutive of the A–B relation
→ C acts back upon and transforms A and B
```

Participatory sense-making ontologizes the interaction more strongly than most theories of intersubjectivity. SIO adds the stronger claim that the relationally generated third is itself subjectivity.

That additional step must not be attributed to participatory sense-making without explicit evidence.

## 12. Primary-source and literature audit required

The following work is required before strengthening or revising the verdict:

1. Verify the original definition of participatory sense-making and interactive autonomy.
2. Identify passages where the interaction process is described as autonomous, normative, or identity-bearing.
3. Audit whether any text explicitly attributes subjectivity or experience to the interaction process itself.
4. Distinguish dyadic agency from dyadic subjectivity.
5. Review later work on we-experience, joint agency, collective intentionality, and social agency within the enactive tradition.
6. Determine whether interactional autonomy meets enactive criteria for organismic autonomy or only a weaker dynamical criterion.
7. Test whether the relational process is pair-specific in the strong identity-preserving sense.
8. Examine whether return influence is symmetrical, reciprocal, and constitutive or merely ordinary causal feedback.
9. Test whether the same generative structure recurs invariantly across bodily, conversational, institutional, and collective scales.
10. Keep explicit doctrine separate from SIO-side reconstruction.

---

## 13. Conditions that would change the verdict

The verdict should move toward an exact match only if primary or authoritative secondary sources establish all of the following:

- two differentiated active subjects jointly generate a third,
- the third is irreducible to the two individual subjects and their externally described coupling,
- the third possesses genuine subjective or experiential status,
- the third is specific to the generating relation,
- it becomes constitutive of the continued A–B relation,
- it acts back upon both participants,
- its identity depends irreducibly on interaction history,
- and the same relational architecture recurs across levels.

Without explicit support for subjective status, the strongest justified classification remains an interactional and relational precedent rather than an exact ontological predecessor.

---

## 14. Provisional conclusion

Participatory sense-making is one of the closest contemporary frameworks to Subjectivity Intersection Ontology.

Unlike theories in which the relation is only a descriptive link between independent minds, it treats interaction as a dynamically organized process capable of generating meaning and reshaping the participants who sustain it. It therefore provides powerful precedent for jointly generated relational organization, history dependence, constitutive mediation, pair specificity, and return influence.

But the available structure currently supports:

```text
irreducible interactional process
```

more clearly than:

```text
irreducible third subjectivity
```

Accordingly, the provisional verdict is:

> **VERY STRONG INTERACTIONAL PRECEDENT, NOT AN EXACT MATCH ON CURRENT EVIDENCE**

The open historical question is whether later enactive work crosses the boundary from autonomous interaction and dyadic agency to a genuinely relationally generated subject of experience.
