# 2. Subjectivity Intersection Ontology

## 2.1 Subjectivity

In this paper, subjectivity does not refer merely to a conscious state, private experience, or psychological interiority. It is defined as a **perspective constitutive of existence**.

A subject is therefore not simply an observer that represents an already existing world. Subjectivity is treated as an ontological condition through which a world exists in a particular mode. From this standpoint, what is ordinarily called objectivity is not an entirely subject-independent domain, but a stabilized structure that has become shareable across multiple subjectivities.

## 2.2 Subjectivity Intersection

Interaction between two or more subjects is common, but **Subjectivity Intersection** does not mean mere contact, communication, causal influence, or information exchange.

Subjectivity Intersection is a **constitutive relation** in which differentiated subjectivities become conditions of one another's existence and jointly generate a new ontological organization. Neither subject merely observes or modifies the other from outside. Each enters into a relation through which the mode of existence of both is transformed.

## 2.3 Emergence of Third Subjectivity

The central hypothesis of SIO is that when a constitutive Subjectivity Intersection is established, a **third subjectivity** may emerge.

This third subjectivity is not an average, sum, representation, coordination mechanism, or descriptive label for the relation between A and B. It is proposed as an irreducible subjectivity that cannot be reduced to either originating subject taken separately.

The crucial distinction is between a relation that merely connects existing subjects and a relation that becomes generative of subjectivity. SIO hypothesizes that, under specific conditions, a relation itself may acquire the ontological status of a subject.

## 2.4 Recursive Reconstitution

The third subjectivity does not complete the process merely by emerging. It acts back upon the originating subjectivities and reconstitutes them.

The structure is therefore not only:

- A → C
- B → C

but also:

- C → A
- C → B

The relation is recursive rather than linear. A and B participate in the generation of C, while C transforms the conditions under which A and B continue to exist. Subjectivity is thus understood not as a fixed substance but as something continuously generated, sustained, and revised through intersection.

A minimal schematic expression is:

- C = F(A, B)
- A' = G_A(A, C)
- B' = G_B(B, C)

where C is jointly generated by A and B, and the resulting C contributes to the reconstitution of both.

## 2.5 Ontological Significance

SIO shares with relational ontology, process philosophy, enactivism, and intersubjective theories the view that relations are constitutive rather than merely external. Its distinctive claim, however, is stronger: relation is not only constitutive **of** subjects but may become constitutive **as** a subject.

The novelty claim is therefore not the introduction of a generic third term. Triads, mediations, syntheses, shared fields, and emergent organizations already appear in many traditions. The proposed novelty lies in the full conjunction of the following structure:

1. differentiated subjectivities remain genuinely distinct;
2. their relation is constitutive rather than merely external;
3. an irreducible third subjectivity is jointly generated;
4. the third subjectivity is not reducible to either origin or to their aggregate;
5. the third subjectivity recursively reconstitutes the originating subjectivities; and
6. the complete structure is treated as an ontological claim open to rejection, rather than only as metaphor, theology, phenomenological description, or functional model.
