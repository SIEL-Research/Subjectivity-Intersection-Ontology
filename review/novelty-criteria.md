# Historical Novelty Criteria

## Status

**VERSION 1.0 — PROVISIONAL AND PUBLICLY AUDITABLE**

These criteria define the structure against which historical candidates are assessed. They are fixed before completion of the literature review to reduce retrospective reinterpretation.

## N1 — Ontological unity

Fundamental being is one.

This criterion is not satisfied merely by methodological unity, epistemic unity, a shared world, a totality composed of parts, or a highest member within a hierarchy. The candidate must posit or entail a genuinely fundamental ontological unity.

## N2 — Internal perspectival differentiation

The one differentiates internally into irreducible subjective perspectives or subjective poles.

The differentiation must not be merely a division into objects, properties, logical moments, symbols, functions, or impersonal processes. It must concern perspective, subjectivity, or an equivalent subjective mode.

## N3 — Emergence of irreducible third subjectivity

A third subjective pole emerges between differentiated perspectives and is reducible to neither.

A relation, field, medium, synthesis, shared object, agreement, common ground, symbolic mediator, or aggregate does not satisfy N3 unless it is explicitly or demonstrably granted subjective status.

## N4 — Simultaneous constitutive closure

The third is both generated by the intersection and constitutive of the relation's completion.

This criterion requires more than a temporal sequence in which A and B first exist independently and later produce C. C must be structurally implicated in the relation through which A and B become determinate as related perspectives.

## N5 — Reflexive action upon the originating perspectives

The third acts back upon, constrains, transforms, or reconstitutes the originating perspectives.

A passive result or static representation is insufficient. The third must have a constitutive or dynamically effective role in the subsequent or simultaneous determination of A and B.

## N6 — Cross-level structural recurrence

The same relational form recurs across ontological levels.

The recurrence need not imply a mathematically proven fractal. It requires a documented repetition of the same structural relation at different levels, such as between fundamental and relative subjectivity and again among relative subjects.

## Exact historical precedent

A candidate qualifies as an exact historical precedent only if all six criteria are explicitly stated or demonstrably integrated within one ontology.

The burden of evidence increases with the strength of the verdict. Similar vocabulary or a loose family resemblance cannot establish equivalence.

## Criterion-level judgments

Each criterion must be marked using one of the following:

- **Explicitly satisfied** — directly supported by primary text.
- **Strongly implied** — not stated in identical terms but strongly entailed by the structure.
- **Partially satisfied** — a significant element is present, but the criterion is incomplete.
- **Interpretive only** — satisfaction depends primarily on reconstruction by this project.
- **Not satisfied** — the candidate explicitly differs or lacks the required structure.
- **Undetermined** — available evidence is insufficient.

## Overall verdict categories

### Exact precedent

All N1–N6 are explicitly satisfied or strongly implied as one integrated ontology, with decisive primary-source support.

### Strong structural match

Most criteria are satisfied, including the central triadic structure, but at least one important difference prevents exact equivalence.

### Partial precedent

The candidate clearly anticipates one or more major components but does not integrate the full structure.

### Interpretive reconstruction

A close structure can be produced only by substantial reconstruction beyond what the source explicitly asserts.

### Superficial resemblance

The candidate shares terms, imagery, or triadic form but not the relevant ontological structure.

### Not applicable

The candidate addresses a different kind of question and cannot meaningfully be assessed against the full criteria.

### Undetermined

The evidence, translation, textual access, or specialist interpretation is insufficient for a responsible verdict.

## Anti-overclaim rule

No candidate may be downgraded merely to preserve novelty, and no candidate may be upgraded merely because of suggestive resemblance. The strongest defensible reading for equivalence must be presented before the final verdict.
