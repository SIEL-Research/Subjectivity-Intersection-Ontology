# Citation and Bibliography Audit

Date: 2026-07-28

## Scope

This audit covers the author-approved manuscript assembled under `manuscript/`. It examines:

- whether historical and contemporary claims in Chapters 1, 4, and 6 identify appropriate sources;
- whether works directly analyzed are distinguished from interpretive and methodological literature;
- whether titles, translators or editors, publication years, publishers, journal data, and persistent identifiers are bibliographically coherent;
- whether canonical divisions can be used in place of edition-specific page numbers for ancient and classical texts; and
- whether every author–date citation has a corresponding reference and every listed reference is used in the manuscript.

The audit does not claim exhaustive coverage of every primary text or specialist interpretation relevant to the traditions reviewed.

## Verification Basis

Bibliographic metadata was checked, where available, against publisher catalogues, DOI records, library catalogues, and disciplinary indexes. Particular attention was given to the editions that determine quoted wording or canonical locators, including the Cottingham–Stoothoff–Murdoch Descartes translations, Tanner's conciliar documents, the Gerson edition of Plotinus, the Olivelle Upaniṣads, the Torella edition of Utpaladeva, and the journal records for Ogden and De Jaegher and Di Paolo. These checks establish the identity of the editions cited; they do not remove interpretive disagreement about the texts.

## Citation System

The manuscript now uses an author–date system compatible with Chicago-style philosophical prose. Ancient and classical texts are cited by conventional divisions where available:

- DK fragment numbers for Parmenides and Heraclitus;
- Stephanus numbers for Plato;
- Bekker numbers for Aristotle;
- treatise, book, and chapter divisions for Plotinus;
- Upaniṣad, sūtra, kārikā, and canonical collection divisions for Indian and Buddhist texts; and
- paragraph numbers for Hegel’s *Phenomenology of Spirit* where relevant.

This permits passages to be checked across translations. The bibliography also identifies the English editions used in the manuscript.

## Substantive Corrections Made

### Descartes

The proposition “I think, therefore I am” is located in Part IV of the *Discourse on the Method*, not stated in that form in the *Meditations*. The Introduction now distinguishes it from the Second Meditation’s formulation “I am, I exist” and cites both volumes of the Cottingham–Stoothoff–Murdoch translation.

### Nicaea and Constantinople

The Nicene use of *homoousios* is cited to the conciliar documents edited by Norman Tanner. The manuscript no longer treats the creed conventionally associated with Constantinople in 381 as a document whose transmission and relation to the council are historically uncomplicated. A qualification and a modern historical source have been added.

### Presocratic Sources

Parmenides and Heraclitus are cited using DK numbering, with both the Diels–Kranz edition and the Laks–Most translation identified. This avoids treating reconstructed modern titles such as *On Nature* as unproblematic bibliographic titles.

### Plotinus

The publication year of the Cambridge Gerson edition of *The Enneads* has been normalized to 2017 in accordance with the publisher's record, and the manuscript cites the work by treatise, book, and chapter rather than by edition-specific pagination.

### Indian Sources

The Upaniṣadic, Sāṃkhya–Yoga, Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika, early Buddhist, Madhyamaka, Jain, and Pratyabhijñā sections now identify textual loci and the editions or translations used. The references distinguish the *Nyāya-Sūtra* from the *Vaiśeṣikasūtra* and do not treat “Indian philosophy” as a single source tradition.

### Contemporary Comparisons

The following bibliographic points were fixed:

- Ogden’s “The Analytic Third” is a 1994 journal article, not a monograph;
- Participatory Sense-Making is cited to De Jaegher and Di Paolo’s 2007 article and its DOI;
- Shani’s 2015 formulation of cosmopsychism and Nagasawa and Wager’s 2016 priority-cosmopsychism chapter are separately identified; and
- the corrected 1978 edition of Whitehead’s *Process and Reality* is specified.

## Result

The reference list contains 80 works:

- 44 primary texts or works directly analyzed; and
- 36 interpretive, methodological, or interdisciplinary works.

Every listed work is used in the assembled manuscript, and every author–date citation introduced in this audit has a corresponding bibliographic entry. The Diels–Kranz edition is invoked through the conventional abbreviation “DK,” which is defined at the beginning of the reference list. OpenAI tools, GitHub, and the public repository are not treated as evidence or included in the philosophical bibliography; their methodological role remains confined to Appendix A.

The audit added documentary support without revising the comparative results. The 21 N1–N6 rows in Chapter 4 remain identical to the corresponding 21 rows synthesized in Chapter 5.

## Remaining Scholarly Risks

The following issues cannot be eliminated by bibliographic normalization alone:

1. Several comparison cases encompass internally diverse textual traditions. The Upaniṣads and Advaita, Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika, early Buddhism, Trinitarian traditions, German Idealism, and Kashmir Śaivism require interpretive reconstruction beyond any single passage.
2. The English version of Gregory of Nyssa used here is historically established but old. A journal or specialist reviewer may request a newer critical translation for detailed textual argument.
3. The Abhinavagupta edition is a multi-volume Sanskrit and English publication whose internal citation conventions should be fixed more granularly if the Kashmir Śaivism discussion is expanded.
4. Schelling and Nishida are represented across distinct phases of development. The present citations support the high-level reconstruction, but a specialist submission may require page-level evidence for each phase.
5. Bibliographic accuracy does not validate the N1–N6 assessments. Those remain interpretive judgments governed by the methodology in Chapter 3.

These points should be treated as limits of the present review rather than as unreported certainty.
