# Search Strategy

## Status

**VERSION 1.0 — DISCOVERY AND CONFIRMATORY SEARCH PLAN**

This document records how historical candidates and relevant scholarship are identified, verified, and logged.

## Search modes

The review separates two modes.

### Discovery search

Purpose: identify potentially relevant thinkers, traditions, concepts, and texts.

Discovery may use broad academic databases, reference works, citation chaining, specialist bibliographies, library catalogues, and AI-assisted exploratory search. Discovery results do not establish equivalence.

### Confirmatory audit

Purpose: test a registered candidate against N1–N6 using primary texts and specialist scholarship.

Confirmatory judgments require traceable citations and explicit separation of source claims from project reconstruction.

## Priority source environments

The search should use, where accessible:

- PhilPapers;
- JSTOR;
- Project MUSE;
- Google Scholar;
- Scopus and Web of Science;
- Oxford Academic;
- Cambridge Core;
- SpringerLink;
- university library catalogues;
- Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy and Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy for orientation only;
- specialist critical editions and primary-text archives;
- bibliographies in peer-reviewed monographs and articles.

## Language coverage

The project begins with English-language scholarship but does not treat English coverage as sufficient for a historical-priority claim.

Priority original languages include:

- Sanskrit;
- German;
- Japanese;
- Greek;
- Latin;
- French;
- Arabic;
- Hebrew;
- Italian;
- relevant vernacular and classical languages identified during the review.

Original-language verification is required when a decisive term is disputed, semantically unstable, or flattened by translation.

## Query recording

Every confirmatory search should record:

- date;
- database or catalogue;
- exact query;
- filters used;
- result count when available;
- sources retained;
- reason for retention or exclusion;
- follow-up search required.

## Citation chaining

For every major candidate:

1. inspect references cited by leading specialist works;
2. inspect later works citing the decisive source;
3. identify disagreements in interpretation;
4. seek scholarship in the original-language research tradition;
5. document whether apparently novel comparisons already exist.

## Search stopping rule

A candidate audit may receive a provisional verdict when:

- the principal primary texts have been identified;
- at least one strong specialist interpretation has been examined;
- the strongest equivalence case has been formulated;
- decisive criteria have sufficient evidence for a bounded judgment;
- unresolved language or textual issues are explicitly recorded.

A provisional verdict is not a claim that all literature has been exhausted.

## Search-log policy

Exploratory notes may contain unverified leads, but any material entering the manuscript or final comparison matrix must carry one of the following status markers:

- SOURCE NOT VERIFIED
- SECONDARY SOURCE VERIFIED
- PRIMARY TEXT VERIFIED
- INTERPRETIVE RECONSTRUCTION
- PROVISIONAL VERDICT

## Integrity rules

- Do not fabricate bibliographic details.
- Do not cite a source not examined.
- Do not treat a search-engine snippet as textual evidence.
- Do not infer absence from failure to locate a source.
- Preserve contradictory scholarship.
- Record abandoned search paths when they affected a judgment.
