# 4. Criteria for Historical Evaluation

## 4.1 Purpose of the Evaluation Criteria

To assess the novelty of Subjectivity Intersection Ontology (SIO), it is not sufficient to discuss similarities and differences with existing ontologies impressionistically. Historical comparison can easily be shaped by the selection of traditions, interpretive assumptions, and differences in terminology.

This study therefore defines the core components of SIO as six explicit evaluation criteria and applies them as a common framework across the philosophical, theological, psychoanalytic, and cognitive-scientific traditions examined in this paper.

These criteria are not intended to rank the value or philosophical importance of the theories under review. Their purpose is narrower: to determine, as transparently as possible, whether SIO is a reformulation of an existing ontology or whether it presents a distinct ontological option.

## 4.2 Evaluation Criteria

### N1. Irreducible Third Subjectivity

Does the theory posit a third term arising in relation to two subjects and treat it not as their sum, average, metaphor, abstraction, or descriptive construct, but as a subject that is irreducible to either of them?

### N2. Generation Through Intersection

Is the third subjectivity defined as something that does not pre-exist the relation, but is generated through the constitutive intersection of two differentiated subjectivities?

### N3. Recursive Reconstruction

Does the emergent third subjectivity subsequently act upon the originating subjects and transform, reorganize, or reconstitute their modes of existence?

### N4. Ontological Reality

Is the third subjectivity treated as an ontological reality rather than merely as an epistemological, psychological, sociological, linguistic, or metaphorical construct?

### N5. General Theoretical Scope

Is the proposed structure formulated as a principle that can apply beyond a particular religion, culture, person, dyad, community, or exceptional case, and potentially extend to subjectivity and existence more generally?

### N6. Complete Structural Conjunction

Does a single theory contain, in a self-consistent and integrated form, the full conjunction of the following elements:

- differentiated subjectivities;
- constitutive intersection;
- emergence of an irreducible third subjectivity; and
- recursive reconstruction of the originating subjectivities?

N6 is not satisfied merely because a theory contains one or more related components. It requires the complete ontological structure proposed by SIO to be present within the theory itself.

## 4.3 Evaluation Procedure

Each theory is evaluated separately against N1–N6.

Partial satisfaction of the criteria must be distinguished from structural equivalence with SIO. For example, a theory may recognize a third term without treating it as generated through intersection, in which case it would not satisfy N2. A theory may emphasize relational constitution without attributing subjectivity or ontological reality to the relation itself, in which case it would not satisfy N1 or N4.

The review therefore distinguishes among:

- clear satisfaction;
- partial or interpretively dependent satisfaction;
- non-satisfaction; and
- insufficient evidence.

This procedure is intended to make disagreements auditable. A reader may challenge an individual judgment without requiring the entire comparative framework to be abandoned.

## 4.4 Similarity Grade

In addition to the criterion-by-criterion evaluation, each theory is assigned an overall Similarity Grade indicating its structural proximity to SIO.

- **S** — Structurally equivalent to SIO in its essential ontological form.
- **A** — Very close to SIO but lacking at least one decisive component.
- **B** — Contains important shared structures but lacks multiple essential criteria.
- **C** — Shows limited or indirect similarity.
- **D** — Is fundamentally different from SIO in the dimensions under evaluation.

The Similarity Grade is a summary judgment and does not replace the individual N1–N6 assessments.

## 4.5 Novelty Verdict

Each theory is also assigned a concise Novelty Verdict describing its historical relation to SIO. Possible formulations include:

- important ontological predecessor;
- triadic predecessor;
- relational predecessor;
- functional predecessor;
- phenomenological predecessor;
- psychological predecessor;
- partial structural analogue;
- no close structural precedent.

These verdicts are not claims of philosophical superiority. They identify the specific historical contribution of each tradition while preserving the distinction between partial anticipation and complete formulation.

The central historical question of this paper is therefore not whether earlier traditions contain ideas resembling SIO. Many clearly do. The question is whether any single prior theory integrates all six criteria into the complete structure of an irreducible third subjectivity generated through constitutive intersection and recursively transforming the subjectivities from which it emerges.
