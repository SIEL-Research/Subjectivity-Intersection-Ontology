# Appendix B

## Open Research Methodology

## B.1 Open Development

This manuscript was developed as an openly documented research project. Rather than remaining private until submission, successive drafts were placed in a public repository and revised through a sequence of recorded changes. The resulting record permits readers to distinguish earlier formulations from later revisions and to inspect how the manuscript's definitions, comparisons, and organization developed.

Open development does not by itself establish philosophical quality, historical accuracy, or the validity of the manuscript's conclusions. Its methodological function is narrower. It makes the development of the research inspectable and reduces the extent to which the final text appears without a visible account of its revision.

The manuscript should therefore be understood as a versioned research object. Individual files record specific stages of composition, while the repository history records when and how those files were added or changed. This arrangement preserves the distinction between a provisional draft, a revised argument, and a version prepared for formal review.

## B.2 Version-Controlled Research

Manuscript development used GitHub as the public environment for version control, draft revision, and the preservation of a transparent historical record. The workflow is organized around several related components:

- **GitHub hosting**, which makes the research materials and repository metadata publicly accessible;
- **version control**, which records changes as discrete commits with timestamps, authorship metadata, and descriptive messages;
- **public issue tracking**, which provides a designated mechanism for documenting questions, proposed revisions, and unresolved problems;
- **draft revisions**, which preserve successive states of individual sections rather than presenting the manuscript only as a completed document; and
- **a transparent historical record**, through which the sequence of development can be reconstructed.

At the present stage, the primary public record in the repository is the commit history and the evolving set of draft files. The public issue tracker remains available for structured review questions and future revision work; it should not be represented as a substitute for evidence contained in the manuscript. Stating this distinction prevents the availability of an issue mechanism from being confused with the existence of a completed public review process.

Version control improves transparency by preserving the sequence between an initial formulation and its later correction or qualification. A reader can identify which claims were introduced together, which sections were revised separately, and whether a later summary corresponds to the underlying comparative work. Descriptive commit messages also make the development of the manuscript easier to audit without requiring every intermediate file to be treated as authoritative.

Version control contributes to reproducibility in a limited sense appropriate to philosophical research. It does not make interpretive judgments mechanically repeatable. It enables another researcher to retrieve a documented version, reconstruct the order of revisions, compare textual states, and identify the materials upon which a particular formulation depended. Stable version identifiers can also clarify which draft is being criticized or cited.

The same record can expose inconsistency, premature conclusions, or unresolved changes. This is a methodological benefit rather than a guarantee of error correction. Transparency makes scrutiny possible; it does not ensure that scrutiny has occurred or that all identified problems have been resolved.

## B.3 Public Review

Historical comparisons, conceptual definitions, and the organization of the manuscript were treated as revisable through publicly documented discussion and subsequent revision. Questions concerning the scope of a comparison, the meaning of an evaluation criterion, or the strength of a novelty claim could therefore be separated from the final prose and addressed through identifiable changes.

Public documentation can make review more granular. A criticism may concern a source interpretation, a criterion assignment, a table entry, or the wording of a conclusion. Recording the resulting revision allows readers to determine which part of the manuscript changed and whether related claims were adjusted consistently. This function parallels the localization of disagreement described in Appendix A.

Public discussion does not replace formal peer review. It may lack anonymous assessment, disciplinary selection, editorial oversight, or systematic engagement by specialists. Participation can also be uneven, and the visibility of a discussion does not establish the competence or representativeness of its contributors.

Open review is therefore treated here as complementary to conventional review. It provides a developmental history before and alongside formal evaluation, while journal review supplies a distinct institutional process of expert assessment. Neither process should be used to imply that the manuscript has been validated merely because comments or revisions are publicly visible.

## B.4 Research Repository

The manuscript development history, draft evolution, review mechanisms, and comparative framework are publicly available at:

[https://github.com/SIEL-Research/Subjectivity-Intersection-Ontology](https://github.com/SIEL-Research/Subjectivity-Intersection-Ontology)

The repository documents the research workflow. Its commit history records successive contributions to the manuscript, and its draft directory preserves the developing sections used in the comparison. Repository metadata can be used to identify particular versions and to distinguish current drafts from earlier states.

The repository is not presented as supporting evidence for the paper's ontological or historical conclusions. Public availability does not verify an interpretation, establish a novelty claim, or confirm SIO. Those claims must be assessed through the arguments, sources, criteria, and limitations stated in the manuscript itself. The repository supports procedural transparency only.

## B.5 Future Development

Both the manuscript and the comparative methodology are intended to remain open to future refinement. Continued philosophical discussion may clarify the definitions of subjectivity, intersection, emergence, and recursive reconstruction. Historical research may identify additional precedents, alternative interpretations, or deficiencies in the present corpus. Mathematical formalization may make structural assumptions and consequences more explicit, while empirical investigation may determine whether operational counterparts of the proposed concepts can be formulated and tested.

Future changes should preserve the distinction between revision history and evidential support. A documented change shows that a proposal has evolved; it does not show that the revised proposal is correct. Where possible, subsequent work should identify the source of a revision, the version affected, and the unresolved questions that remain.

Open development is thus used as a method for preserving provenance, enabling criticism, and coordinating future work. Its value depends upon accurate records, explicit limitations, and continued separation between the documentation of research and the justification of its conclusions.
