# Subjectivity Intersection Ontology
## Toward a Relational Theory of Emergent Third Subjectivity

## Abstract

The rapid development of artificial intelligence, cognitive science, and neuroscience has renewed the classical question of what a subject is. Yet the basic ontological options that support this question have not been fundamentally expanded for roughly two millennia. Monism, dualism, and later developments such as non-dualism and relational ontology have each yielded important insights, but the question of whether a relation itself can exist as a subject has not been sufficiently formulated as a systematic ontology.

This paper proposes **Subjectivity Intersection Ontology (SIO)** as a new ontological framework for addressing this problem. SIO is based on the hypothesis that when two differentiated subjectivities enter into a constitutive relation, an irreducible **third subjectivity** emerges from their intersection, and that this third subjectivity recursively reconstitutes the two originating subjectivities. Relation is therefore not treated merely as a structure connecting pre-existing subjects, but as an ontological condition capable of generating, sustaining, and transforming subjectivity itself.

To clarify the historical position of this proposal, the study introduces six evaluative criteria (N1–N6) and applies them comparatively to Kashmir Shaivism, Christian Trinitarian ontology, Schelling, Hegel, Nishida Kitarō, Whitehead, Merleau-Ponty, participatory sense-making, the analytic third, and cosmopsychism. While each of these traditions shares important structural features with SIO, the present review has not identified an exact historical precedent that integrates all six conditions.

This paper does not present SIO as an established ontology. Rather, by clarifying its conceptual structure and situating it historically, it presents SIO as a **falsifiable research program** open to further philosophical, mathematical, and empirical investigation.
