# Subjectivity Intersection Ontology

Open development, systematic prior-art review, critical audit, and formal construction of Subjectivity Intersection Ontology.

> We do not begin by claiming historical priority. We begin by making that claim falsifiable.

## Research purpose

This repository openly documents the development of Subjectivity Intersection Ontology (SIO), including its core definitions, historical prior-art review, strongest counterexamples, revisions, rejected interpretations, and manuscript preparation.

The central research question is:

> Has any prior philosophical, theological, mystical, phenomenological, psychoanalytic, process-philosophical, cognitive-scientific, or consciousness-theoretical framework explicitly integrated all six structural conditions defined in this repository into one ontology?

## Core ontological proposal

The provisional core proposal is:

1. Fundamental being is one, but not internally undifferentiated.
2. The one differentiates internally into distinct subjective perspectives.
3. An irreducible third subjectivity emerges between differentiated perspectives.
4. The third is both generated by the intersection and constitutive of the relation's completion.
5. The third acts back upon, constrains, or transforms the originating perspectives.
6. The same relational structure recurs across ontological levels.

The operational canonical form is:

```text
AB → C → AB
```

This is not intended as simple temporal causation. It denotes a simultaneous, self-consistent closure in which A, B, and C become mutually constitutive within one relation.

## Six novelty criteria

The historical audit evaluates candidate traditions against six criteria:

- **N1 — Ontological unity**
- **N2 — Internal perspectival differentiation**
- **N3 — Emergence of irreducible third subjectivity**
- **N4 — Simultaneous constitutive closure**
- **N5 — Reflexive action upon originating perspectives**
- **N6 — Cross-level structural recurrence**

See [`review/novelty-criteria.md`](review/novelty-criteria.md).

## What this theory is not

Subjectivity Intersection is not defined as:

- an average of two subjects;
- consensus or majority agreement;
- simple information integration;
- imitation by one subject of another;
- fusion or identity of two subjects;
- a set-theoretic common part;
- an externally added mediator;
- an observed proxy mistaken for subjectivity itself.

## Historical novelty status

**Current status: UNDER INVESTIGATION**

No claim of historical unprecedentedness is presently established. The repository is designed to expose that claim to falsification by identifying, reconstructing, and testing the strongest possible precedents.

## Contribution and criticism policy

Contributions are especially welcome when they:

- identify a stronger historical precedent;
- provide primary-text evidence in the original language;
- challenge an N1–N6 judgment;
- distinguish explicit doctrine from interpretive reconstruction;
- expose ambiguity, overclaim, or category error;
- propose a clearer formalization or empirical boundary.

Negative findings and rejected interpretations will be preserved rather than erased.

## Methodological separation

The project keeps four levels distinct:

1. **Ontology** — claims about absolute, relative, and intersection subjectivity.
2. **Operator hypothesis** — proposed formal representations of projection, perspective transformation, and simultaneous closure.
3. **Observable consequence** — measurable traces such as synchronization, reciprocal change, novel meaning, or joint generation.
4. **Decision rule** — criteria for support, non-support, or non-testability.
