# 8 Limitations

## 8.1 Scope of the Historical Review

The historical review intentionally concentrates on major ontological traditions and representative formulations that are directly relevant to the structural features of Subjectivity Intersection Ontology (SIO). It does not attempt an exhaustive survey of every historical ontology, nor does it claim comprehensive coverage of every period, region, school, or interpretive development within the traditions selected.

This restriction is methodologically necessary for a focused comparative study. The traditions examined provide a broad range of contrasts concerning substance, process, relation, consciousness, plurality, emergence, and subjectivity. They are sufficient to establish a structured initial comparison, but they do not exhaust the philosophical record.

Additional traditions may warrant future examination. Further work could extend the comparison to underrepresented philosophical lineages, additional schools within traditions already discussed, and theories whose relevance becomes clearer as the SIO framework is refined. Such expansion may identify further structural precedents, revise individual judgments, or disclose categories of comparison not captured by the present review.

The principal historical conclusion must therefore remain bounded by the corpus examined. The study reports that no complete structural equivalent was identified among the reviewed theories; it does not infer universal absence from philosophical history as a whole.

## 8.2 Interpretation of Historical Sources

Philosophical interpretation is not unique. Historical texts admit competing readings, and the meaning of technical concepts often depends upon linguistic, argumentative, and institutional contexts. This is particularly important when concepts such as subject, self, person, relation, consciousness, and emergence are compared across periods and traditions in which they perform different functions.

Different scholars may therefore reasonably disagree about the application of N1–N6 to particular theories. A third term may be interpreted as ontological by one reader and as methodological, symbolic, or functional by another. Similarly, a relation described as constitutive within one interpretive framework may appear derivative within another. Judgments of partial satisfaction are especially sensitive to these differences.

The explicit evaluative framework is intended to localize rather than eliminate such disagreements. A reader can dispute the reconstruction of a tradition, the assignment of one criterion, the overall Similarity Grade, or the Novelty Verdict without rejecting every other comparison. This makes the judgments more auditable while preserving the role of philosophical interpretation.

The framework does not remove the need for primary-text analysis, philological care, or engagement with specialist scholarship. Its reliability depends upon the adequacy of those prior interpretive tasks. Accordingly, the comparative matrix should be read as a structured summary of the interpretations defended in this manuscript, not as a replacement for the historical arguments on which those interpretations depend.

## 8.3 Evaluation Criteria

N1–N6 are introduced in this paper as one possible comparative methodology. They were designed for a specific research question: whether a single theory integrates differentiated subjectivities, constitutive intersection, an irreducible emergent third subjectivity, recursive reconstruction, ontological reality, and general theoretical scope.

Because the criteria derive from the structure being evaluated, they do not constitute a neutral taxonomy of all ontologies. They illuminate proximity to SIO but do not measure the overall scope, coherence, explanatory power, or historical significance of another theory. A low similarity result indicates structural distance under these criteria, not philosophical deficiency.

Several methodological questions remain open. The criteria may overlap, N6 is explicitly conjunctive, and partial satisfaction can reflect different forms of proximity or uncertainty. The transition from criterion-level judgments to an overall Similarity Grade is qualitative rather than algorithmic. These features allow historically sensitive evaluation, but they also leave room for disagreement about weighting and classification.

Future work may refine, extend, or replace the criteria. Revised definitions could distinguish functional from ontological similarity, separate restricted from general scope, or assign different forms of confidence to interpretive judgments. Alternative comparative methodologies may reveal relations not visible within N1–N6. The present framework should therefore be evaluated by the clarity and usefulness of the comparisons it supports rather than treated as a final method for comparative ontology.

## 8.4 Conceptual Rather Than Empirical Contribution

This paper does not empirically validate Subjectivity Intersection Ontology. Its contribution is conceptual, historical, and methodological. Empirical evaluation remains future work.

At the conceptual level, the paper defines the proposed structure and distinguishes constitutive intersection from aggregation, external relation, and descriptive third terms. At the historical level, it compares that structure with selected ontological traditions. At the methodological level, it makes the grounds of comparison explicit through N1–N6, Similarity Grades, and Novelty Verdicts.

None of these contributions demonstrates that an irreducible third subjectivity occurs in natural, social, neural, or artificial systems. Historical distinctness would not establish ontological truth, and conceptual coherence would not by itself establish empirical instantiation. Conversely, the absence of current empirical validation does not decide the conceptual or historical questions addressed by the paper.

Future empirical work would require operational definitions that distinguish coordination, emergent organization, and collective behavior from subjectivity. It would also require models, measurements, controls, interventions, and falsification conditions appropriate to the domain under investigation. Until such work is developed, SIO should not be treated as an empirically confirmed theory.

## 8.5 Future Work

The limitations identified above define several directions for further research:

- **Refinement of comparative ontology.** Future studies may clarify the criteria, distinguish different kinds of partial similarity, formalize the relation between criterion-level judgments and overall grades, and compare the method with alternative forms of historical analysis.
- **Mathematical formalization.** Formal models may specify relations among differentiated subjects, an interaction process, and a candidate emergent third. Such models should make assumptions and failure conditions explicit without equating formal variables with subjectivity.
- **Empirical investigation.** Research in social cognition, multi-agent systems, and interpersonal neuroscience may provide domains in which operational consequences of the framework can be examined. Evidence of interaction or synchrony must remain distinct from evidence of emergent subjectivity.
- **Broader historical comparison.** Additional philosophical traditions, primary texts, and specialist interpretations may extend or revise the present matrix and test the robustness of the provisional historical assessment.

These tasks are not all prerequisites for the present paper's limited conceptual and historical contribution. They define a future research agenda corresponding to questions that the current study deliberately leaves open. Progress in one domain will not automatically settle the others: historical comparison, conceptual analysis, mathematical representation, and empirical testing provide distinct forms of assessment.

The limitations therefore specify the boundaries within which the present conclusions should be read and the conditions under which those conclusions may be strengthened or revised. They are best understood as an agenda for continued research rather than as weaknesses that must all be resolved before the proposed framework can be examined philosophically.
