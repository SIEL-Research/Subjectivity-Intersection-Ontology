# Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

## Status

**VERSION 1.0 — REVIEW ELIGIBILITY RULES**

These criteria determine which sources and candidate theories enter the historical novelty audit.

## Inclusion criteria

A candidate theory, tradition, text, or research program is included when at least one of the following applies:

1. It posits a fundamental ontological unity together with internal differentiation.
2. It develops an explicitly triadic account of subjectivity, relation, mediation, generation, or return.
3. It attributes irreducibility or partial autonomy to a third term arising between two poles.
4. It proposes that a relation or third term acts back upon its originating participants.
5. It treats intersubjectivity, interaction, community, or shared meaning as constitutive of subjects rather than merely external to them.
6. It addresses the emergence of multiple subjects from one fundamental subject or consciousness.
7. Specialist scholarship has identified it as structurally close to relational monism, triadic ontology, the analytic third, participatory sense-making, cosmopsychism, or a comparable framework.
8. A credible critic or contributor proposes it as a possible counterexample to the historical novelty claim.

## Source inclusion criteria

Sources may enter the evidential record when they are:

- primary texts or critical editions;
- reliable scholarly translations;
- peer-reviewed articles;
- specialist academic monographs;
- chapters in scholarly edited volumes;
- dissertations with substantial primary-text analysis;
- reputable academic reference entries used for orientation;
- archival or historically significant commentaries relevant to interpretation.

Exploratory sources may be logged without being treated as decisive evidence.

## Exclusion criteria

A candidate is excluded from full N1–N6 audit when it is based only on:

- generic use of three-part symbolism;
- a simple thesis-antithesis-synthesis formula detached from the candidate's actual texts;
- metaphorical unity without ontological commitment;
- a third object, variable, mediator, observer, or aggregate with no subjective status;
- consensus, averaging, information fusion, or shared representation alone;
- a purely epistemological account that makes no claim about the constitution of subjects or being;
- a purely methodological analogy with no historical or textual evidence;
- unsourced internet summaries, promotional material, or generated text;
- terminological resemblance unsupported by structural analysis.

Exclusion from full audit does not imply intellectual insignificance. It means only that the candidate does not presently bear on the defined novelty question.

## Treatment of uncertain cases

When relevance is plausible but evidence is incomplete, the candidate remains registered as **Undetermined** rather than being excluded.

Uncertain cases must record:

- the unresolved criterion;
- missing primary texts or translations;
- competing interpretations;
- the evidence needed to change the verdict;
- whether specialist review is required.

## Primary-text verification policy

A claim central to an exact-precedent verdict should be verified in a primary text whenever possible.

Verification must record:

- author and work;
- edition or translation;
- book, chapter, section, proposition, page, or other stable locator;
- decisive original-language term where relevant;
- the project's interpretation;
- credible alternative interpretations.

A secondary source may support a provisional judgment but cannot replace primary-text verification when the alleged equivalence turns on a disputed concept.

## Translation policy

Multiple translations should be compared when a decisive term may mean, for example:

- subject rather than substance;
- perspective rather than representation;
- relation rather than mediator;
- emergence rather than manifestation;
- identity rather than unity;
- return rather than reciprocal action;
- field or place rather than subjective pole.

The project must not force source terminology into SIO vocabulary without documenting the interpretive step.

## Strongest-counterexample rule

Any candidate reasonably capable of satisfying all six criteria must be included even when it threatens the project's novelty claim.

The review must first state the strongest case for equivalence before presenting differences.

## Duplicate and derivative sources

Derivative summaries are not counted as independent evidence. Where several sources reproduce the same interpretation, the earliest or most authoritative source should be identified.

Distinct scholarly interpretations of the same primary text should be retained when they materially alter the N1–N6 assessment.

## Temporal boundary

A historical precedent must predate the first public fixation of the relevant SIO structure. Later theories may be important comparisons, developments, independent convergences, or critiques, but they cannot establish earlier historical priority.

The precise public-fixation date and evidence will be recorded separately in the project chronology.

## Final inclusion record

Every audited candidate should have:

- an inclusion rationale;
- a primary-source list;
- a secondary-source list;
- criterion-level judgments;
- strongest case for equivalence;
- strongest case against equivalence;
- provisional or final verdict;
- unresolved questions;
- revision history.
