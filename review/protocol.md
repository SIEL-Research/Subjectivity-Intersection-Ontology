# Historical Prior-Art Review Protocol

## Status

**PREREGISTERED-STYLE WORKING PROTOCOL — VERSION 1.0**

This protocol governs the historical and cross-traditional review of Subjectivity Intersection Ontology (SIO). It is intended to prevent retrospective adjustment of novelty criteria and to preserve a transparent record of judgments, revisions, negative findings, and unresolved cases.

## Research question

Has any prior philosophical, theological, mystical, phenomenological, psychoanalytic, process-philosophical, cognitive-scientific, or consciousness-theoretical framework explicitly integrated all six structural conditions of Subjectivity Intersection Ontology into one ontology?

## Review objective

The review does not begin by assuming historical originality. Its purpose is to identify the strongest possible precedents and determine whether they constitute:

- an exact historical precedent;
- a strong structural match;
- a partial precedent;
- an interpretive reconstruction;
- a superficial resemblance;
- or an unresolved case.

## Unit of comparison

The unit of comparison is not isolated vocabulary, metaphor, triadic symbolism, or general resemblance. Each candidate theory is assessed against the complete structural set N1–N6 defined in [`novelty-criteria.md`](novelty-criteria.md).

## Scope

The initial review includes, but is not limited to:

- Indian philosophy and Sanskrit traditions;
- German-language philosophy and mysticism;
- Japanese philosophy;
- Neoplatonism and emanationist traditions;
- Christian Trinitarian theology;
- Jewish and Islamic mystical traditions;
- process philosophy;
- phenomenology and theories of intersubjectivity;
- semiotics and theories of irreducible thirdness;
- psychoanalytic theories of the third;
- enactivism and participatory sense-making;
- contemporary panpsychism and cosmopsychism;
- social ontology, collective intentionality, and group agency.

The scope may be expanded, but additions must be documented in the version history.

## Review workflow

1. **Discovery search**  
   Identify candidate traditions, thinkers, concepts, and texts through broad searches and reference chaining.

2. **Candidate registration**  
   Record each serious candidate before final judgment.

3. **Strongest-case reconstruction**  
   Formulate the strongest defensible argument that the candidate may satisfy N1–N6.

4. **Primary-source verification**  
   Verify decisive claims in primary texts whenever accessible. Use original-language consultation where translation materially affects the judgment.

5. **Counter-analysis**  
   Identify the strongest reasons why the candidate may fail one or more criteria.

6. **N1–N6 assessment**  
   Assign criterion-level judgments with explicit evidence and uncertainty.

7. **Provisional verdict**  
   Classify the candidate using the verdict categories defined in the novelty criteria.

8. **External criticism**  
   Preserve objections, alternative readings, and corrections.

9. **Revision record**  
   Any changed judgment must retain the previous verdict and explain the evidential basis for revision.

## Evidence hierarchy

Evidence is ranked as follows:

1. Primary text in the original language.
2. Reliable scholarly translation of a primary text.
3. Specialist peer-reviewed scholarship.
4. Reputable academic reference works.
5. General secondary summaries.
6. Exploratory notes or AI-assisted discovery.

Lower-ranked evidence may identify candidates but should not alone establish an exact historical precedent.

## Explicit doctrine and reconstruction

Every comparison must distinguish among:

- what the source explicitly states;
- what a specialist interpretation reasonably attributes to it;
- what this project reconstructs by analogy;
- what remains unknown.

A candidate cannot be classified as an exact precedent solely through interpretive reconstruction.

## Treatment of negative findings

Negative results are part of the research record. A failed equivalence claim, absent primary-text support, or rejected interpretation must be retained with its reasons.

Absence of evidence in a limited search is not evidence of historical absence. Claims such as “no precedent exists” require bounded language and a documented search scope.

## Decision boundary

An exact historical precedent must explicitly or demonstrably satisfy all six criteria N1–N6 as one integrated ontological structure. Mere possession of a triad, a doctrine of unity, a theory of relation, or a concept of emergence is insufficient.

## Amendments

The review protocol may be amended only through a dated, versioned change that states:

- the previous rule;
- the revised rule;
- the reason for revision;
- whether prior candidate judgments require reassessment.
